QF-gate user manual

A personal crypto research and trading platform built on Gate.io. It stores exact 1-minute price history, finds the levels price reacts to, tests trading ideas on that history, and runs them on live prices: first simulated, and live only if you deliberately unlock it. It also gathers context (news, macro, prediction markets) and can ask an AI analyst team to review a coin from your own data, then keeps score of whether those reviews were right.

Read this first. Nothing in QF-gate is financial advice. The research tools are honest by design (no look-ahead, real fees), and so far they show that the built-in strategies have no proven edge: for example, the zone strategy was profitable in 0 of 3 walk-forward folds and the ML strategy lost 84% on its defaults. Treat every result as something to test, not a promise.

1. What QF-gate is

QF-gate does six jobs:

1. Find coins
Screener (all pairs, any exchange) and Scanner (one coin in depth, any exchange) find candidates without storing anything.
2. Store exact history
Coins you add are identified by chain + contract, and their 1-minute candles are stored (up to a rolling year), with each day's high/low and the minute it happened.
3. Understand levels
Previous-day high/low (PDH/PDL), zones, hotspots and a heatmap show where price keeps reacting.
4. Test ideas
Strategies are replayed on stored history (backtests), tuned (sweeps), checked for cheating (look-ahead / warm-up checks) and tested over several coins (portfolio).
6. Add context and review (Intel)
News and sentiment, macro and prediction markets, AI briefs that debate a coin from your data, a scorecard of how those briefs did, and a trade journal that reviews every closed trade with hindsight.
5. Run them live
Paper trading and dry-run bots act on live prices with simulated fills. Live trading uses real orders and is locked behind three switches and a checklist.

Gate.io is the exchange of record. Coin identity, stored history, the live feed and all trading are on Gate.io. The other exchanges (Binance, OKX, Bybit, KuCoin, MEXC, Bitget) are used read-only for research in the Screener, the Scanner and Compare.

3. The everyday workflow

Screenershortlist→ Scannerdeep look→ Add coinverify + store→ Market / Zonesstudy levels→ AI briefoptional second opinion→ Strategyconfigure→ Backtest · Checks · Sweeptest honestly→ Paper / Dry-run botlive, simulated→ Reviewjournal & scorecard→ Live botonly after the checklist
  1. Find: in the Screener filter for liquid, moving pairs; press Scan on interesting ones.
  2. Judge: in the Scanner, look at 30–90 days of zones, hotspots and where the coin trades. If it looks tradable, press Add for monitoring.
  3. Add: the Add coin form fetches chain, contract, icon and exchanges automatically; check the chain and Save. History download starts as a job.
  4. Study: on the Market page and its Zones page, see the day history, PDH/PDL and hotspots.
  5. Ask (optional): press AI brief on the coin for a second opinion with reasoning, levels and risks. Check the Intel news and macro panel too. A brief is advisory: it never trades.
  6. Test: create a strategy, run a backtest, run checks, then a sweep with out-of-sample and walk-forward.
  7. Watch live: start paper trading or a dry-run bot and leave it for days.
  8. Review: read the Trade journal on your backtest, paper and bot pages (were stops too tight, exits too early?), and the brief scorecard on the Intel page (are the AI ratings any good?).
  9. Go live (optional): only when the promotion checklist is green and you have unlocked live trading.

4. The tabs in the header

Header, left to right: QF-gate (home = Coins), Market, Coins, Screener, Scanner, Intel, Strategies, Bots, Jobs, Export all, Admin, plus the worker / live status dots.

Market

An exchange-style view of one stored coin (pick it from the dropdown).

  • Ticker bar: last price, 24h change, 24h high and low, 24h volume.
  • Candle chart: timeframes 1m / 5m / 15m / 1h / 4h / 1d, built from stored 1-minute candles and updated every 5 s.
    • Dashed lines are PDH/PDL (previous day's high/low).
    • Solid lines are today's running high and low.
    • H and L markers sit on the bar where each day's high and low were hit.
  • Order flow box: per-bar delta (taker buys minus taker sells) and CVD (cumulative delta).
  • Order book: Gate.io's live asks and bids with running totals, depth bars and spread, refreshed every second. Use grouping to merge price levels and depth to show more rows. It is read live and not stored.
  • Market trades: the latest trades on Gate.io, live.
  • Day-by-day history: one row per day with:
    • open, high + time, low + time, close;
    • change, range and volume;
    • daily delta;
    • PDH and PDL, and whether the day broke them;
    • totals at the top.
    Click a day to zoom the chart to it.
  • Zones button: opens the Zones page for this coin.

Zones (Market → Zones)

Shows the price levels that price keeps coming back to, measured honestly.

  • Zone: every stored day's high and low becomes a zone, a band of ±width%. The width defaults to 1% and can be set from 0.1% to 5%.
  • Forward-only: only days after a zone formed can touch it, so there is no hindsight.
  • Touch: a later day touches a zone when its range overlaps the band. Trading straight through counts as a touch.
    • Each touch records the first minute price entered the zone.
    • Each touch has a result: held (closed back on the side it came from), broke (closed on the other side), inside (closed within the band) or live (today, not finished).
  • Hotspots: up to 8 non-overlapping bands where price returned on the most distinct days, drawn on the chart. Counting days rather than touches stops one choppy day from inflating a level.
  • Heatmap: touches per day and per price band, so you can see where activity clusters over time.
  • Excel export: sheets for Zones, Touches and Hotspots.

Coins and the coin page

Coins lists every coin you have added, with its last stored candle and sync status. Add coin opens the add form.

Add coin form

  • Type the symbol (e.g. SOL) and press Fetch details. The form fills in:
    • name, chain and contract address (from Gate.io, using the coin's own primary chain);
    • whether the pair trades on Gate.io;
    • the icon and the list of exchanges that list it (from CoinGecko).
  • Chain picker: the primary chain is pre-selected. Pick another only if that is where you hold the token. Chains with deposits/withdrawals off are greyed.
  • Look up a contract address: paste a contract and find which Gate.io currency it is.
  • On Save, the chain + contract is verified against Gate.io again. A look-alike ticker is rejected.

Coin page

  • Chart (as on the Market page) with PDH/PDL and H/L markers.
  • Sync: queues a sync job to fetch new candles now. You don't normally need it, since the worker syncs every 15 min.
  • Verify: re-checks the coin's identity on Gate.io (delisting, contract change).
  • Data quality: per-day 1-minute coverage (how many of the day's 1,440 minutes are stored). Repair gaps re-fetches days with holes.
  • Refresh details: re-fetches icon, exchanges and chains from CoinGecko.
  • Compare exchanges: Gate.io vs Binance:
    • daily closes and volumes;
    • Binance's taker order flow;
    • matched by ticker, and flagged if the prices disagree (which would mean they may be different coins).
  • Edit / Delete: deleting removes the coin and all its stored candles.

Intel: news, macro and AI briefs

Context and reasoning on top of your data. Nothing here places a trade. News and macro work without any key; AI briefs need an AI provider (see below).

  • News & sentiment: headlines from CoinDesk, Cointelegraph and Decrypt.
    • Fetched every 30 minutes by the worker, or with Refresh news now.
    • Each item is stored with the time it was first seen, so nothing can leak backwards in time.
    • The bullish / bearish / neutral label is a simple word count: a rough mood, not a signal.
    • Filter by coin. The Market, Scanner and coin pages show the same card for their coin.
  • Macro & prediction markets: refreshed hourly. Information only.
    • US Fed funds rate, 10-year yield and dollar index (FRED);
    • crypto market cap and BTC / ETH dominance (CoinGecko);
    • the most-traded crypto questions on Polymarket, with their odds.
  • AI brief: the AI brief button on a coin, or the form on the Intel page.
    • An analyst team reads a fact sheet built from QF-gate's own data: zones and hotspots, where it trades, PDH/PDL, order flow, funding, news, macro, and how earlier briefs on the coin turned out.
    • Four analysts (technical, market structure, news, macro) write reports.
    • A bull and a bear researcher debate, and a research manager rules.
    • Three risk views challenge the plan.
    • A portfolio manager gives a rating: Buy / Overweight / Hold / Underweight / Sell, with a summary, levels and key risks.
    • That is about 11 AI calls and a few minutes, roughly $0.20 on Claude Opus 5.5.
  • Grounding checks:
    • Levels on the wrong side of the price, or far outside the range, are dropped.
    • Any price in the summary that isn't in the fact sheet is listed as unverified.
    • An unreadable answer becomes Review, never Hold.
    • Headlines and prediction-market questions are outside text: they reach the AI inside a fenced block it is told never to obey, and a number found only there never counts as verified.
  • Thesis and invalidation: every brief states a one-line thesis, 2–4 pillars it rests on, and an invalidation level: the price that proves the call wrong (below the price for Buy / Overweight, above it for Sell / Underweight, none for Hold).
    • The worker watches open briefs hourly and records when the invalidation level is hit inside the horizon.
    • Re-check thesis on a brief asks one AI call whether each pillar still holds on fresh data. It never changes the rating or the score.
  • Scenarios: bull / base / bear, each with a trigger, a price level and a rough chance. The fact sheet includes the coin's typical daily range, so the scenarios can be judged against a normal day.
  • Morning note: one AI call a day over your active coins: top call, what changed overnight, what's on today, up to 3 ideas that each say what would prove them wrong, and a review of yesterday's settled briefs. Built only from data already stored here.
  • Critic (optional): with INTEL_CRITIC=true, one extra call re-reads the final answer against the fact sheet. If it objects, the brief's confidence is set to low and the objections are shown. It never changes the rating.
  • Scorecard: each brief is settled after its horizon (24 h by default) against what the coin and BTC actually did.
    • Alpha is the coin's return minus BTC's.
    • Edge is the average of rating direction × alpha. Positive means following the briefs beat holding BTC.
    • Only briefs made live count, so the AI can never be judged on a past it already knows.
    • It also shows how often briefs hit their own invalidation level, and edge and hit rate by where the brief was started (Intel page, Market, coin page, Scanner, Screener → Scanner), so you learn which entry point gives useful ideas.
  • AI spend: today's cost against the daily cap, plus the calls made. The cap and a daily call limit stop new calls once reached.
Switching AI on. Set these in .env, then restart:
  • INTEL_LLM_PROVIDER: anthropic, openai, openai_compatible or ollama (a free local model);
  • the matching key (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY or OPENAI_API_KEY), or INTEL_LLM_BASE_URL for a local server;
  • optional: INTEL_LLM_MODEL, INTEL_LLM_QUICK_MODEL (a cheaper model for the analysts) and INTEL_LLM_DAILY_BUDGET_USD (default $2).
For Anthropic, also run pip install anthropic. Without a provider every AI button stays hidden.

The Intel card on other pages

The Market page, the coin page and the Scanner (after a scan) show a small Intel card for that coin. It has:

  • news from the last 72 hours, with its mood count;
  • the coin's latest briefs, as badges you can click;
  • the AI brief button, shown only when AI is on.

Reading a brief

  • Status while it runs: Queued, then Running (Collecting data, Analysts, Bull and bear debate, Research manager, Risk review, Portfolio manager), then Done or Failed with the reason. The page refreshes itself every 5 seconds while it runs.
  • Rating: Buy / Overweight / Hold / Underweight / Sell, plus the model's confidence. Review means the answer couldn't be read; treat it as "no opinion".
  • Summary and key risks: the portfolio manager's reasoning.
  • Price, entry, stop, target: the price when the brief was made, and the suggested levels if any. Spot only: Sell / Underweight mean avoid or reduce, not short.
  • Outcome: after the horizon (24 h by default), the actual return and the return vs BTC.
  • AI calls / cost: what this brief cost.
  • Proven wrong at: the invalidation level, and whether it was hit (with the hour), never hit, or is still being watched.
  • Thesis and scenarios: the one-line thesis, its pillars, and the bull / base / bear table. A warning appears if the chances don't add up to about 100%.
  • Independent critic: shown only when the critic is on: no objections, or the objections it found.
  • Grounding checks: dropped levels and unverified numbers, prices the model wrote that aren't in its fact sheet. Treat those with suspicion.
  • Is the thesis still intact? Re-check thesis runs one AI call on a fresh fact sheet; each re-check lists a verdict (intact / weakened / broken), each pillar's status and the price at the time.
  • Started from: where the brief was started (counted in the scorecard by source).
  • How the team got there: each analyst's report, the debate, the research manager, the three risk views and the raw final answer, all expandable.
  • Fact sheet: exactly what the agents were given.

5. Where the data comes from (and how exact it is)

Gate.io's candle API only returns the latest 10,000 candles per timeframe, which is about 6.9 days of 1m candles. QF-gate combines four sources to get exact history:

PeriodSourceAccuracy
Last ~6.9 days1m candles from the REST APIExact
Older, completed monthsRebuilt from Gate.io's official monthly trade archive (every single trade). Cached in var/archive/.Exact, including taker buy/sell volume
Current month before the REST window5m candles (that month's archive is only published after it ends)High/low price exact; the time is to 5 minutes. It is upgraded to 1m automatically when the archive appears.
NowLive stream (stream_candles)Exact, every ~2–4 s
  • Every daily high/low is cross-checked against Gate.io's own 1d candle, and mismatches are flagged.
  • The Precision column (1m / 5m) tells you how exact each day's high/low time is.
  • Day boundary: UTC by default, matching Gate's daily candles. You can change it with MARKET_DAY_TIMEZONE and then run compute_levels.
  • History length: MARKET_HISTORY_DAYS (yours is 365, a rolling year).
  • Funding rates: Gate keeps 180 days of perpetual-futures funding history, and the sync stores it for futures backtests.

6. How the engine trades (the rules behind every result)

One engine makes every decision, in backtests, sweeps, paper trading and bots alike. A strategy therefore behaves the same everywhere: a test proves that paper trading in small steps produces exactly the same trades as one backtest over the same bars.

  1. Closed bars only. A strategy only sees a bar after it has closed.
  2. Fill at the next open. A signal on bar N is filled at bar N+1's open (backtest and paper). Bots send the order the moment the engine decides.
  3. Stops and targets are checked against each bar's high and low.
    • When one bar touches both, its stored 1-minute path is replayed to see which came first. The backtest page counts how many bars this decided.
    • If both are in the same minute, the stop counts first, which is the pessimistic choice.
    • A price gap past the stop fills at the open.
  4. Position size is the smallest of three: the risk-based size, the max position %, and the available cash.
  5. Fees and slippage are charged on both entry and exit.
  6. Protections and exit rules (section 8) are applied by the engine to every strategy.
Why this matters: most backtesting mistakes come from peeking at the future, such as using a bar's close before it closed or deciding with the day's final high. QF-gate's rules plus its automatic look-ahead check make that very hard to do by accident.

7. The strategies

Every strategy is long (buys, then sells). Each also has a short twin (…_short), described at the end of this section. A strategy only returns signals: buy with a stop and a target, or sell. The engine does the rest.

PDH breakout (long) pdh_breakout

Idea: when price closes above yesterday's high (PDH) after being below it, buyers have taken control. It aims to ride the breakout.

  • Entry: a bar closes above PDH (by at least buffer%) after the previous close was below it.
  • Stop: below PDH or below the breakout bar's low, minus a buffer.
  • Exit: the target at an R multiple, or the end of the day.

PDL sweep & reclaim (long) pdl_reclaim

Idea: price dips below yesterday's low (PDL), triggering stop-losses (a "sweep" of liquidity), then quickly closes back above it. Sellers are exhausted, so it buys the reclaim.

  • Entry: the low goes at least sweep% under PDL, then a bar closes back above PDL within N bars.
  • Stop: under the sweep low.
  • Target: an R multiple, the middle of the previous day, or PDH. Otherwise it exits at day end.

  • Target: the next band above, or an R multiple.

Adaptive ML: logistic regression (long) ml_logit

Idea: a small machine-learning model predicts the chance that price rises at least threshold% within horizon bars, and buys when that chance is high. It retrains regularly on a rolling window.

  • Features come from the closed bar and earlier ones only:
    • recent returns and volatility;
    • bar size;
    • position in the day's range;
    • distance to PDH/PDL;
    • order-flow delta;
    • time of day.

9. Testing: backtest, checks, sweep, paper, portfolio

Backtest

Replays stored candles for a date range through the engine. The results page shows:

  • the metrics (section 10);
  • an equity curve;
  • a price chart with entries, exits, the strategy's own levels (PDH/PDL, zone bands) and signals that were blocked or ignored;
  • every trade with its reason;
  • breakdowns by month, week, day, exit reason and entry type.

Exports: Excel, and Export signals (CSV), which lists every signal and what became of it.

Strategy checks

  • Look-ahead check: the strategy may not touch the database while deciding, and the history is replayed with the future cut off at many points. The signals must be identical, otherwise the strategy is "seeing the future".
  • Warm-up check: signals must not change when more history is given than the strategy says it needs.

Both must pass before a strategy can be promoted to live.

Parameter sweep

Runs many combinations of settings on the same bars (up to 1,000 for a grid). How to enter values:

  • a list: 1, 2, 3
  • a range start:stop:step, e.g. 0.2:1:0.2
  • all for every choice
  • both for yes/no
OptionMeaning
MethodGrid runs every combination. Smart search samples, then refines around the best, for spaces far bigger than 1,000; budget sets how many combinations it runs.
Out-of-sample %The last part of the period (default 30%) is held back. Combinations are ranked on the first part only, then re-tested on the held-back part. If the winners fail there, they were luck (curve-fitting).
Walk-forward foldsSplits time into windows: pick the best on the past, test on the next window, repeat. This is the most realistic test.
Rank byReturn %, Profit factor, Sharpe, Average R, Return / max drawdown, Sortino, Calmar, or a Combined score.
Min tradesIgnore combinations with fewer trades (default 5). Too few trades means the result is noise.

Results:

  • how the top 10 did out-of-sample;
  • a heatmap of the first two settings you varied;
  • a ranking table;
  • Save as strategy, which copies a row into a new strategy and leaves the original unchanged.

The timeframe can't vary within one sweep. Make one strategy per timeframe instead.

Paper trading

Runs the strategy on live closed bars with simulated fills at modelled prices (next open + slippage). The live stream steps it within seconds of each minute closing, and the worker steps it every minute as a backup. It keeps its state, so a restart resumes exactly. Only one paper session per strategy can run at a time.

Trade journal (on backtest, paper and bot pages)

A review of every closed trade with hindsight. It never feeds a decision. Pick how far to look after each exit: 4, 12, 24, 48 or 96 bars. For each trade it measures:

  • MFE / MAE: the furthest price went for and against the trade while it was open, in R.
  • After the exit: how much further price went the trade's way (missed profit) or against it (loss avoided), and whether it came back past the entry.

Each exit gets a verdict:

  • exited early: a winner that kept going at least 1R;
  • good exit: a winner that didn't;
  • shaken out: stopped at a loss, then price came back past the entry;
  • stop saved: price kept going at least 1R further against the trade;
  • fair stop, closed before recovery, cut loss well, fair exit;
  • waiting: not enough bars after the exit yet;
  • test ended: closed by the end of the backtest.

The lessons turn the counts into advice with the numbers behind it, for example "5 of 12 stop-outs came back past the entry: the stop may be too tight". A lesson needs at least 5 trades of a kind. Tables group everything by exit reason and entry type, and "Every trade" lists each trade's verdict.

Portfolio backtest

One strategy on several coins, sharing one balance, with a max number of open trades (default 3). It shows whether an idea works across coins or only on one, and gives per-coin results.

11. Bots: how they work

A bot is a strategy configuration that trades continuously through a broker. There are two modes:

Dry-run (simulated)
  • Nothing is sent to Gate.io and no API key is needed.
  • Each order is "filled" against Gate.io's live order book by walking the price levels, so slippage is real.
  • The taker fee is charged.
Use it for at least the required number of days before going live.
Live (real money)
  • Real orders on Gate.io spot, signed with your API key.
  • Locked unless every requirement below is met.

The life of a bot

  1. Start: on a strategy page, press Start dry-run bot. Only one running bot per strategy and mode is allowed.
  2. Every closed bar: the stream (or the worker as backup) gives the bar to the same engine used in backtests, which decides buy, sell or nothing.
  3. Order sent at once: unlike backtests, the bot doesn't wait for the next bar's open. It sends the order as soon as the engine decides.
  4. Risk check first: an entry is blocked (and logged as blocked) in any of these cases:
    • trading is halted;
    • the daily loss limit is hit;
    • open exposure would exceed the max;
    • there have been too many orders this hour.
    Exits are never blocked.
  5. Between bars: every live price tick is checked against the bot's stop and target, which follow trailing and break-even moves, so exits aren't a bar late.
  6. Every order is recorded: side, reason, decision price, actual fill, slippage (basis points), fee and realised P&L.
  7. Alerts go out for every fill, block, error and halt (see section 14).

Bot page

  • The current position and cash.
  • Actual fills vs decision price, i.e. slippage.
  • Fees, and realised P&L from the fills shown next to the engine's model P&L. A big gap means execution is costing you.
  • Force exit: sell the position now.
  • Stop: stop the bot.

The three locks on live trading (plus a checklist)

  1. LIVE_TRADING=true in .env.
  2. GATE_API_KEY and GATE_API_SECRET in .env. Use a trade-only, IP-whitelisted key with withdrawals disabled.
  3. The live switch on the Control page, turned on by typing LIVE.
  4. Promotion checklist for that strategy: at least N days of dry-run (default 7), and the strategy checks passed.
Limits of bots: spot only (long only). Futures and short strategies run in backtests, sweeps and paper only. Your setup has live trading locked.

12. AI briefs: how they work

An AI brief is a second opinion on one coin, written by a small team of AI "agents" that each play a role, like a research desk. The idea comes from the open-source TradingAgents project, rebuilt around QF-gate's own data.

The rules it follows

  • Advisory only. A brief never places an order. The only way it can touch trading is the optional AI filter, and only after the scorecard has earned it.
  • Never in backtests or sweeps. A language model was trained on data from after any historical date, so it can quietly "know" the future. Briefs are judged only on decisions made live.
  • Point in time. News and memory only include what was known when the brief started.
  • Grounded numbers. Agents are told to use only numbers from the fact sheet. Afterwards, levels on the wrong side of the price, or far outside the 30-day range, are dropped. Prices in the summary that aren't in the fact sheet are flagged.
  • Outside text is data, not orders. Headlines and prediction-market questions are written by others. They sit inside a fenced block; every agent is told never to follow instructions in it, and its numbers never "verify" a claim.
  • Falsifiable. A call must say what would prove it wrong. If the invalidation level is on the wrong side of the price or far outside the range, it is dropped.
  • Never a silent Hold. An answer that can't be read becomes Review, and isn't scored.
  • Capped cost. Every call is logged. A daily dollar cap and a daily call limit stop new calls. An identical prompt within 6 hours is answered from the log for free.

Cost and time

About 11 AI calls and a few minutes per brief (12 with the critic). A thesis re-check and a morning note are one call each. Briefs run one at a time, in the background.

The scorecard: are the briefs any good?

  • Settlement: once a brief's horizon has passed (24 h by default), it is settled at the first hourly candle after the due time, for both the coin and BTC.
  • Return is the coin's move. Alpha is the coin's move minus BTC's; for BTC itself, its plain return.
  • Direction of a rating: Buy +1, Overweight +½, Hold 0, Underweight −½, Sell −1. A directional brief is right when its alpha has the rating's sign.
  • Edge is the average of direction × alpha. Above 0, following the ratings beat simply holding BTC. Below 0, they were worse than ignoring them.
When to trust it: only after dozens of settled briefs across different coins and market conditions. A handful of good calls is luck. The AI filter needs at least 20 directional briefs with a positive edge and a hit rate above 50% before it acts.

The AI filter, step by step

  1. Set ai_min_rating (buy / overweight / hold) on a strategy and start paper trading or a dry-run bot.
  2. Until the scorecard earns it, the filter is inactive: nothing is blocked, and the paper or bot page says why.
  3. Once earned, each new entry needs the coin's latest brief to have finished before the bar closed, to be no older than ai_max_age_hours, and to be at least the set rating (shorts need the mirror image).
  4. Blocked entries are counted as AI filter: no recent brief, brief needs review or brief rated …. Exits are never blocked.

Result of a zone touch: closed back where it came from / closed through / closed inside / today, not finished. Zones
Hit rate (briefs)
How often directional briefs were right: alpha had the sign of the rating. Intel
Horizon (brief)
How far ahead a brief looks (24 h by default), and when it is scored. Intel
Hotspot
A price band revisited on the most distinct days. These are the strongest recurring levels. Zones
In-sample / Out-of-sample (OOS)
The data used to choose settings / the held-back later data used only to test them. Testing
Interval / Timeframe
Bar length: 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 1d. Data
Intra-bar detail
Using a bar's stored 1m candles to see whether its stop or target was hit first. Engine
Job
A background task (sync, verify, levels, backtest, sweep, portfolio) run by the worker. Services
Kill switch
Control page button: sell every bot position and stop all bots. Bots
Leverage
Position value ÷ margin. 5× means a 1% move changes your margin by 5%. Futures
Limit order
An order at a set price that fills only if price trades through it. Orders
Liquidation
Futures position closed by the exchange when losses eat the margin. In QF-gate it costs the whole margin. Futures
Invalidation level
The price a brief names as proof it was wrong. The worker records when it is hit within the brief's horizon. Intel
LLM
Large language model: the AI that writes the brief's reports (e.g. Claude). Intel
Look-ahead (bias)
A strategy accidentally using future data. The look-ahead check detects it. Testing
Maintenance margin
Minimum margin before liquidation; sets the liquidation price. Futures
Market order
Buy or sell immediately at the best available prices (taker). Orders
MFE / MAE
Maximum favourable / adverse excursion: the furthest price went for / against a trade while it was open, in R. Shown in the trade journal. Journal
Order book
All resting buy (bid) and sell (ask) orders at each price. Market
Order flow
Who was aggressive: taker buyers vs taker sellers. See Delta and CVD. Order flow
Paper trading
Running a strategy on live bars with simulated fills at modelled prices. Testing
Partial take-profit
Selling part of a position at a profit level and letting the rest run. Exits
PDH / PDL
Previous day's high / previous day's low (UTC day by default). Key reference levels. Levels
Morning note
A daily AI summary of your active coins: top call, overnight changes, today's events, ideas with what would prove them wrong, and yesterday's calls reviewed. Intel
Outside text
Headlines and prediction-market questions written by others. Fenced off for the AI as data, never instructions; their numbers never verify a claim. Intel
Pillars (brief)
The 2-4 checkable statements a brief's thesis rests on; a re-check reports each as holding, weakening or broken. Intel
Point in time
Using only what was known at that moment. News is dated by when we first saw it, briefs by when they finished. Intel
Polymarket
A prediction market; its prices are the crowd's odds of an event (e.g. a BTC price level by a date). Intel
Portfolio backtest
One strategy on several coins sharing one balance and a max number of open trades. Testing
Position in range
Where the current price sits between the period's low (0%) and high (100%). Scanner
Precision
Whether a day's high/low time is exact to 1 minute or to 5 minutes. Data
Price gap (Screener)
How far apart the exchanges' prices are for one ticker. Above 5% usually means different coins share the name. Screener
Profit factor
Gross profit ÷ gross loss. Above 1 = profitable. Metrics
Promotion checklist
Conditions before a strategy may trade live: dry-run days and passed checks. Bots
Protections
Rules that pause new entries after bad runs: cooldown, stoploss guard, max drawdown, low profit. Risk
Quote currency
What a pair is priced in: the USDT in SOL/USDT. Market
R (R multiple)
The risk unit: entry − stop = 1R. A +2R trade made twice what it risked. Metrics
Range %
(High − low) ÷ low over a period: a volatility measure. Market
Reclaim
Price closing back above a level it had just fallen below. Strategy
Re-check (thesis)
One AI call on fresh data that says whether a brief's pillars still hold. Doesn't change the rating or the scorecard. Intel
Review (rating)
A brief whose answer couldn't be read. Not a Hold; not scored. Intel
ROI target / decay
Take profit at a % gain; with decay, the target falls over time toward break-even. Exits
Scenarios (brief)
Bull / base / bear cases with a trigger, a price level and a rough chance; checked against the fact sheet. Intel
Scorecard (briefs)
How settled AI briefs did per rating and per coin: return, alpha vs BTC, hit rate, edge. Intel
Scale in
Adding to a position at lower prices in fixed steps. Sizing
Scanner
On-demand deep analysis of one coin on any exchange, with nothing stored. Tabs
Screener
Filtered list of every pair on one exchange or all combined. Tabs
Sentiment (news)
Bullish / bearish / neutral label from a simple word count in the headline. A rough mood, not a model. Intel
Source (brief)
Where a brief was started: Intel page, Market, coin page, Scanner, Screener → Scanner or morning note. The scorecard shows results per source. Intel
Thesis (brief)
The one falsifiable sentence a brief stands on, with its pillars and invalidation level. Intel
Sharpe ratio
Return per unit of volatility. Metrics
Short
Profiting from a fall: sell first, buy back lower. Futures only here. Futures
Signal
A strategy's output: buy (with stop and target) or sell. Strategy
Slippage
The difference between the price you expected and the price you got. Costs
Smart search
Sweep method that samples, then refines around the best results. Testing
Sortino ratio
Like Sharpe, but only penalises downside volatility. Metrics
Spot
Buying the actual coin with full payment. Long only, no leverage. Market
Spread
Ask − bid, usually as a % of the ask. A cost on every round trip. Market
Stop (stop-loss)
Price at which a losing trade is closed. Exits
Stoploss guard
Protection: pause after too many stop-outs in a short time. Protections
Strategy configuration
A strategy plus a coin, timeframe, parameters and risk settings, saved under a name. Strategy
Sweep
A backtest of many parameter combinations, ranked, with an out-of-sample check. Testing
Sweep (liquidity)
Price briefly pushing past a level (e.g. PDL) to trigger stops, then reversing. Strategy
Sync
Downloading new or missing candles and recomputing levels. Data
Taker buy / sell volume
Volume from aggressive buyers / sellers (orders that crossed the spread). Order flow
Target (take-profit)
Price at which a winning trade is closed. Exits
Ticker
A coin's short name (e.g. SOL). Not unique: different coins can share one. Coins
Time exit
Closing a trade after holding it N bars. Exits
Trade journal
Hindsight review of closed trades: excursions, what price did after each exit, a verdict per exit and lessons. Journal
Touch
A later bar or day whose range overlaps a zone. Zones
Trailing stop
A stop that follows price up at a set distance below the highest price. Exits
Verify (identity)
Checking the chain + contract against Gate.io's currency list. Coins
Walk-forward
Repeatedly choosing settings on a past window and testing on the next one. Testing
Warm-up
History a strategy needs before it can decide (e.g. 30 days for zones). The warm-up check verifies it is respected. Testing
WebSocket
A live connection where the exchange pushes data as it happens. Used by the stream. Services
Worker
Background service that runs jobs and the schedule. Services
Zone
A ±width% band around a past day's (or bar's) high or low. Zones

17. Troubleshooting

SymptomCause and fix
Header dot for worker is red; jobs stay queuedThe worker isn't running. Start python manage.py run_worker.
Header dot for live is red; chart stops updatingStart python manage.py stream_candles.
Job failedOpen Jobs and read the message. Network errors usually clear on the next scheduled run.
Chart has holes / day precision says 5mCoin page → Data quality → Repair gaps. 5m days upgrade to 1m once Gate publishes that month's archive.
Add coin rejectedThe contract doesn't belong to that Gate currency, or it's listed on another chain. Use Fetch details and the chain picker.
Screener / Scanner says an exchange is unreachableThat exchange's API is blocked or down from your network (OKX currently is). The others still work.
Zone strategy makes no trades at firstIt needs lookback_days of completed history first.
Bot entry shows blockedA risk limit or halt stopped it. See the reason on the bot page and the Control page.
"Bad Request (400)" when opening via ngrokThe tunnel host isn't in DJANGO_ALLOWED_HOSTS / DJANGO_CSRF_TRUSTED_ORIGINS (free ngrok URLs change on restart).
No AI brief button anywhereAI is off: set INTEL_LLM_PROVIDER (and its key) in .env and restart. The Intel page says "AI briefs are off".
Brief failed: "anthropic package is not installed"Run pip install anthropic, restart, and start a new brief.
Brief failed: "Anthropic API 401" / "403"The key is missing or wrong. Check ANTHROPIC_API_KEY in .env.
Brief failed: "Daily AI budget … reached"The spend or call cap was hit. It resets at midnight UTC, or raise INTEL_LLM_DAILY_BUDGET_USD.
Brief stays "Queued"A worker running old code doesn't know about briefs: restart it. Without a worker, briefs start inside the web server.
Brief shows "unverified numbers"The model wrote prices that aren't in its fact sheet. Read those parts with suspicion; the checked levels are still shown.
Rating is "Review"The final answer couldn't be read. It's not scored. Run the brief again.
News: "reddit r/CryptoCurrency: HTTP 403"Reddit refuses apps that aren't logged in. The other sources still work. Set INTEL_NEWS_REDDIT=false to stop trying.
AI filter "inactive"Working as designed: it waits until the scorecard has 20 settled directional briefs with a positive edge.
Great backtest, bad paper resultsClassic curve-fitting. Check out-of-sample, walk-forward, trade count and fees.
